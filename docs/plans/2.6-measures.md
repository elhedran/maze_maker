# Slice 2.6 step 2: the measures

## The command

```
cargo run --release -p maze-core --example measures -- [--walks N] [--steps N] [--seed N]
```

Defaults: 6 walks of 40 steps, world seeds 1 to 6, about 25 s. Counts are seeded and repeat exactly. Times are for the machine the report runs on. The measure functions are in `crates/maze-core/src/world_measures.rs`, with tests. `world.rs` gains one block at the end of `impl World` (`random_step`, `has_way_onward`, `memory`).

## Definitions (Rowan's two-way doors, accepted by Yew)

- **Explorer walk:** each step the player jumps to the stored cell beside a random open endpoint, and the world grows around it. The plain random walk (`World::random_walk`) is reported too, but barely grows the world (about 17 patches and 547 cells after 40 steps), so the explorer is the stress case.
- **Frontier:** open endpoints are walkable sides facing an unsettled cell; outward ones are those rule 6's flood reaches from the rim. Sampled at the start and after every step (an explorer step grows about 6 patches). "Closed" counts steps with no outward endpoint. The per-patch guarantee is a step 1 test.
- **Pressures:** 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25 and 0.1. The loader allows nothing above 1.
- **Room:** room-kind cells joined where both facing sides are floor or floor wall. A door is not a join. "On edge": a room cell on its patch window's outer ring. The baseline is the room share of stored cells by distance from the window's edge. No room spans two patches (checked; rule 1 implies it).
- **Connectivity:** walkable means not hedge-kind ([DR-0030](/decisions/0030-measures-for-2-1.md)). Reachable from the origin in a grown world, from DR-0030's centre in 2.1. A dead end has one joined neighbour and no open endpoint. Loops are joins − cells + 1 over the reachable area.
- **Forced worst case:** an estimated upper bound from measured parts, not a forced run, since nothing provokes a contradiction. It takes the slowest patch from small worlds and the check time from the largest, so it is conservative. Spine and fallback stages are forced for the timing rows.
- **Memory:** estimated from allocated capacity; the map's node overhead is approximated.

## Wren's judgement (`game-designer/1`)

1. **Frontier: pass.** "There is always somewhere new to go." Lower pressure only makes the world bigger: at 0.1 it stores 1.7 times as many cells and sprawls, and "too many loose ends lose the feel of a hedge maze". **Two-way door: the world's opening pressure stays at 1** through step 2, reviewed on screen in step 3. 2.3's value is for pockets in a fixed-size maze, a different problem. If step 3 shows the way on getting thin (the minimum was 2 outward endpoints), try 0.75 first.
2. **Rooms against patch edges: watch in step 3, don't raise P.** The ring is one cell wide and patches sit on open endpoints, not a grid, so a player would see a corridor margin round rooms, not stripes. Room sizes are good. If rooms line up in a pattern on screen, the recorded fix is a larger P.
3. **Connectivity: pass.** It answers the 2.1 finding, but by rule 4's guarantee rather than as something that emerged. 1.5 dead ends per 100 cells is "cozy"; the game may later want a few more, as places for rewards.
4. **A walker that heads for the frontier: yes**, for step 3. It plays like a curious player. Keep some doubling back, so the world is seen when the player turns round.
5. **Definitions: fair**, with one watch. The loop count includes room interiors (a 3 × 3 room adds 4), which is probably why grown worlds (27.8) and 2.1 (28.5) read the same. For step 3, count loops with each room as one node. Small, and it blocks nothing.

## Yew's check against DR-0036 (`architect/1`)

All five items pass; no overrules; no new record.

1. **The measures measure what was asked.** The forced stages really are forced. The rule 6 timing calls the same `has_outward` every attempt runs. Sampling the frontier per step is fine. Two wording fixes to the report, made by Rowan: the worst case counts 5 checks, and it says it mixes scales.
2. **Concern 1: accept "leaning pass, judge on screen in step 3".** P = 32 halves rooms touching an edge but leaves the ring dip unchanged, at four times the time, so a larger P doesn't fix the symptom measured. The dip is mostly rule 1's edge masks and anchors sitting on the ring. The recorded answer stays a larger P if Wren and Damson see seams.
3. **Concern 2: pass for 2.6.** The incremental rule 6 check is deferred to the game, the engineers' call. **Revisit trigger, whichever comes first:** the first game slice that grows a world (the incremental check goes in its definition of done), or any walk in step 3's tool where the check takes longer than the median patch (about 25k stored cells, a box of about 66k cells).
4. **Memory: leave the growth log as it is** (Yew's two-way door). `Patch.stored` is load-bearing: the regenerate-a-patch replay test uses it, and so will step 3's last-patch overlay and per-patch deltas. About 31 MB a million cells is fine for 2.6. **Revisit before the game first saves a world**, so the save can hold only the anchors.
5. **No conflict with DR-0036.** Its Consequences get dated notes: rooms (P = 32 didn't move the ring effect) and data that grows (the trigger above).
