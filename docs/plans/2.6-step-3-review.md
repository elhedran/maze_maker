# 2.6 step 3 review

Reviewed from Ash's material (no display): three worlds from the real `maze-core` with `core.toml` (seeds 1, 2026 and 424242, 300 seeded random steps each), ASCII maps with patch seams and growth logs, and screenshots of the real app replaying the recorded responses. Records: [DR-0036](/decisions/0036-growth-from-open-endpoints.md) (growth, P = 16, r = 8, pressure 1), [DR-0039](/decisions/0039-world-view-design.md) (the view), [DR-0015](/decisions/0015-tile-set.md) and [DR-0017](/decisions/0017-tile-glyphs-and-colours.md) (tiles and glyphs), and the [roadmap](/roadmap.md), 2.6.

## Wren

### Verdict

**Passes for the demo. Nothing in the view blocks it.** Worlds 2026 and 424242 read as a hedge maze worth wandering: winding corridors, junctions every few cells, small rooms dotted about like garden pavilions, and a ring of outward arrows at the rim saying "there's more this way". Replay matches. The one thing to change now is which seed Ian demos with, not code.

### (a) The view, for Ian's demo

1. **Demo with a seed that grows (2026 or 424242), not seed 1.** Seed 1's origin patch has only two ways on, both 9 or more cells west of where the random walker wandered, so 300 steps grow nothing and Replay walk stays disabled. The world isn't stuck: walking west with the arrow keys reaches an arrow and it grows. But a first look at "Play, and nothing happens" would read as broken. **Change now:** the demo script (DR-0039, "How Ian demos it") names a seed that grows, or says "if nothing grows, walk toward an arrow". Not a code change. Damson may want a status hint such as "no open endpoint within 8 cells"; that is her call, and not a blocker.
2. **Patches that store 1 to 4 cells under a 16 × 16 outline** look odd but are honest (see b2). Not a blocker. Damson owns how Last patch shows them.
3. **The ○ legend entry is almost never seen** (see b4). Harmless. Damson's call whether to keep it in the legend.

Everything else Ash listed (the fold, replay not following, the status line mid-replay, the room tint past east walls) is how the view reads, so it is Damson's.

### (b) The growth

1. **Rooms against patch edges (Yew's concern 1): no visible grid.** Rooms are complete inside the patch that made them and sit wherever that patch put them. Seams often run along a room's wall, because a later patch wrapped round it, but that reads as a hedge around a pavilion, not a grid. Rooms are small (1 × 2 up to 3 × 4) against P = 16, so the window edge barely constrains them yet. **Goes to 2.6 step 2's room-size and edge measure**, as planned. Revisit at 2.3 if tuning grows rooms toward P, when the edge will start to bite.
2. **Many patches keep 1 to 4 cells.** These are mostly anchors in gaps inside the explored area (2026: patches 3, 6, 7, 9, 11, 14; 424242: 6, 9, 11, 12, 20, 21). The patch fills a little nook and closes it. For the player that's good: a short side passage that ends near where they've been, not a sprawl. It costs patches, not experience. **Goes to step 2's per-patch measures** (add cells stored per patch, so we know how much growth is "filling nooks" against "pushing outward"). No change now.
3. **The frontier is healthy once a world gets going, and thin at the start.** 22 to 34 endpoints at the end of the walk, all outward, at the rim. That's the "way onward" promise holding, with plenty of choice. But seed 1's origin patch left only two, both on one side: a player spawning there has a single direction to go and a lobe of dead ends to the east. A cozy start should offer choice within a few steps. **Goes to step 2's frontier measure** (count endpoints after the origin patch across many seeds, and their spread by direction) **and to 2.3's opening-pressure tuning**, which is exactly the lever for this: pressure below 1 discourages pieces that close every side they have, so more ends stay open. I'll judge the start in 2.3 with that count beside the maps.
4. **Non-outward endpoints almost never appear.** That follows from rule 2: any endpoint within r grows at once, so an enclosed nook is filled as soon as the player is near, and few are left dangling. For the player it's good news: very few unreached closets. No action; step 2's frontier measure already counts them.
5. **The hedge tile is never stored.** That follows from rule 4: the hedge tile has no walkable side, so it is never joined to an anchor and never kept. Every hedge in a grown world is an unsettled cell. The player can't tell (unsettled is drawn as solid hedge), so this isn't a problem for 2.6. It matters for two things:
   - **2.3 tuning.** The hedge tile's weight no longer puts hedge in the world directly. It only shapes what the run around it looks like before the discards. 2.1's "more hedge" lever (from the [2.1 review](/plans/2.1-maze-review.md)) won't do what it did. I'll tune with that in mind. **Goes to 2.3.**
   - **Yew's concern 3,** below.
6. **Yew's concern 3, a cell drawn solid that later opens: it happens, and it is visible in this view.** Every small "nook" patch in b2 turns hedge-drawn cells inside the explored area into path, often within a few cells of where the player walked. With S off you can watch a green patch of hedge become a corridor. Sealed gaps (every neighbour shows hedge) never change; only unsettled cells in a pocket that reaches an open endpoint can. In this tool it's expected and fine: r = 8 and a 40-cell view mean growth happens on screen (Damson's concern on DR-0039). **For the game it would break trust.** A player who has seen a hedge and later finds a path through it feels the maze is shifting under them, which is the opposite of a hedge maze you can learn. **Revisit at G.1 (Walk a growing world)**, when the game's field of view is set: r must cover every cell the player can see *and* every unsettled pocket reaching into it, or seen unsettled cells must be fixed as hedge (stored as the hedge tile) the moment they're seen. That second option is a DR-0036 change and Yew's to weigh; I'd ask for it if the measure shows pockets reaching into view. **Goes to step 2 as a measure:** over a walk, how many cells stored by a patch were within distance r + 4 of the player at an earlier step while unsettled. That number tells us whether concern 3 is a corner case or a pattern.

### What would change my mind

- If step 2 shows that most seeds start with two or fewer endpoints, the start is a design problem, not a seed quirk, and it should go to 2.3 ahead of other tuning.
- If the concern 3 measure is near zero at a game-like r, I'd drop the "store seen hedge" ask.
